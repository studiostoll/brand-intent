What a brand means.
Authored, structured, shared.

Brand Intent is an open file format for the reasoning behind a brand: its voice, its rules, what it never says, where it is and when it is open. I wrote it in my studio while running brands with clients, and I publish it to be read, adopted and argued with. Nine plain-text file types, readable by designers and by the AI agents they now work with.

01 / One post, and the files that made it

Every line on the canvas comes from a file someone can read and change.

  1. The bread name is the primary slot of an example in daily-bread.purpose, which says what a daily bread post is for and limits the name to 30 characters.
  2. Bold, 6 % of the canvas, autofit up to 10, line height 1.15 is typography headline in krume.brand. Flour on dark roast is the Chalkboard theme in the same file, and the dark scrim under the text stops at the 40 % its photo-look allows.
  3. Row 3, all four columns, left is one line in editorial.composition, which knows nothing about bakeries.
  4. 1080 × 1350, five rows by four columns is the 4:5 format, with the margins Instagram crops in the profile grid.
  5. Grams, a flour, hours obey krume.identity: weight in grams on every bread, the flour by name, fermentation in hours, and never “artisan”.
  6. Hands, dough, flour, no smile into the camera is imagery in krume.identity (always real work steps and daylight, never bread as decoration on linen) and framing bakehouse in krume.brand, whose photo-look takes 10 points of saturation out.
  7. Tomorrow morning today.story posts the same purpose with {weekday} as the badge and {sunrise} and {closes} in the caption, from the calendar, the sky and weserstrasse.place.

Rendered in CSS from the Krume files, Chalkboard theme. Wordmark set in Mansalva, an open face standing in for the brand’s display face.

02 / The problem

The reasoning behind a brand is rarely written down.

Brand teams write vision, mission, values, then jump to the visual identity. The reasoning in between, why this typeface, why never that word, why this audience first, lives in someone’s head, a workshop deck, or page 47 of a PDF nobody opens after launch. Guidelines are fuzzy by design: “be authentic”, “use a warm tone”. Two designers reading the same guideline produce two different things, both arguably on brand.

The problem is older than AI. AI made it fast. A designer working from fuzzy guidelines compensates with taste and context. An agent reaches for the generic, and it does so faster than anyone can catch it. Brand Intent gives the reasoning a structure that is concrete enough to test against, and plain enough that a person, a parser and a language model read the same file.

03 / The framework

Nine file types in three groups: what a brand says once, what it posts, and what is true about it.

Each file type has one job and answers one question. A downstream file never repeats what an upstream file has settled: a purpose narrows the identity and does not restate it. The separation keeps the reasoning intact while the expression changes.

04 / The files

Nine file types, in plain text. Here is a line or two from each.

All excerpts are from the Krume bakery, the fully commented reference that ships with the package. Each heading links to the whole file.

01

Identity krume.identity

The strategic core: essence, promise, positioning, named narratives with one marked primary. Voice with machine-checkable always and never rules, and instead pairs that teach the rewrite. Values as behaviour statements with a line to say and a line not to. Imagery rules and image themes. Goals with a share and a question each. Review questions asked before anything goes out. Nothing visual, nothing compositional. 

voice
  register:  informal, Du
  persona:   The baker behind the counter: direct, dry, proud of the craft
  never:
    - lifestyle language (curated, hand-picked, moment of indulgence)
    - "artisan" or "artisanal": we say craft
  instead:
    - "the finest ingredients" -> Tipo 1 and rye 1150 from the mill in Dachau
    - "baked with love" -> eighteen hours of fermentation
    - "fresh from the oven" -> on the shelf since six

review
  copy:
    - Does the text name the bread, the flour, the weight or the fermentation time?
    - Does it sound like the baker behind the counter?
  image:
    - Does the picture show a real step of the work or real customers?
    - Is the bread food, not decoration?
02

Brand krume.brand

The derived expression: colours and the themes that pair them, fonts and named typography styles, spacing, dividers, badges, icons. Since this autumn also the brand’s place in the world: a location for sunrise and weather, a camera, named framing modules for photos, and a photo-look. Every value traces back to a sentence in the identity. 

framing counter
  horizon:     45%
  description: The counter at eye level, the street through the shop window behind it.
  goals:       kiez, service

photo-look
  saturation: -10      # flour, crust and wood, not a food blog
  warmth:     6        # the light of the bakery, early
  contrast:   5
  scrim:      40%      # under light text on a photo, at most this dark
03

Format instagram-4-5-feed-portrait.format

The canvas: platform and post type, size in pixels or millimetres, grid, and three safe zones from the outside in. Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, print and signage. The one brand decision in the file is which purposes are editorially valid here. 

size:     1080 1350
grid:     5 4           # 5 rows × 4 columns
crop:     0 34 0 34     # px - profile grid crops top/bottom
comfort:  40            # px - safe reading margin
purposes: daily-bread, seasonal-special, baking-tip
04

Purpose daily-bread.purpose

What one kind of post is for. Semantic slots (primary, secondary, detail, meta, label, cta, credit) with a typography style, a colour and a length each. A scope that narrows the identity, a context with only what is specific to this content type, a default goal and framing. And examples: whole pages, one topic each, the shape AI writing has to produce. 

context
  primary is the bread name, 1-3 words (e.g. "Rye loaf").
  secondary is the weight and price ("750 g · 5,80 €").
  meta is one sentence: what makes this bread specific today.
  Never describe the bread as "delicious" or "special" -
  state a fact (flour type, fermentation time, seasonal ingredient).

example
  topic: Butter stollen, baked from the first Sunday of Advent
  when:  advent
  label:     Advent only
  primary:   Butter stollen
  secondary: 1 kg · €24
  meta:      Butter from Brandenburg, matured for three weeks. On the shelf from 27 November.
05

Composition simple-stack.composition

Where the slots go. Grid mode places them in rows and columns; flow mode stacks them in panels with sticky bars, spacers and dividers. Photos and illustrations are placed here too. A composition holds zero brand-specific information and is reusable across brands. 

sticky: bottom s
  | logo right

flow: panel bottom-left 2/3 1/1
  | spacer-auto
  | label left
  | primary left
  | divider-line
  | secondary left
  | spacer-s
  | meta left
  | spacer-auto
06

Story today.story

What the brand posts, page by page. Each page names a purpose and a composition. A slot can be fixed, open, or hold a variable: the date, the weekday, {sunrise} and {sunset} from the brand’s coordinates, {weather} from a forecast, {opens} and {closes} from a place, {occasion} and {days-until} from the brand’s time, {person} and {since} from a person. What is not known is not guessed: without a forecast, on a day the place is closed, or for a person whose file has no name, the variable has no value and the post doesn’t go out. Page lists repeat a page as often as the week needs. A story can ask for a photo taken now, with the brand’s camera settings. 

id:     today
format: 4:5
place:  weserstrasse

page
  purpose:     daily-bread
  composition: editorial
  label:       {weekday}
  meta:        Light since {sunrise}, oven on since four. Open until {closes}, what’s gone is gone.
07

Place weserstrasse.place

A site the posts speak about: a shop, a café, an information office. Address, coordinates, a context line for AI writing, how many people fit, and the regular weekly hours. A day not named is closed. Holidays are not part of the file. 

address:   Weserstraße 17, 12045 Berlin
context:   Former copy shop with one counter and three breads a day. Regulars at seven, sold out by noon on Saturdays.

hours
  tue-fri: 07:00-14:00
  sat:     07:00-13:00
  sun:     closed
  mon:     closed

capacity
  indoor:  6
  outdoor: 8
08

Time krume.time

When the brand lives. Its founding, its seasons and the windows a seasonal post fits, its week (the Saturday bread, the Thursday tasting), its dates (the open yard on 2 May, the anniversary) and its day: the oven on at four, everything on the shelf at six. A story or an example names one of these with when: and is only offered in season. 

founded: 2019-05-04

weekly wheat-blend
  label:   Wheat blend
  days:    sat
  context: 500 g, only on Saturdays, sold out by ten.

daily
  oven:  04:00  Oven on
  shelf: 06:00  Everything on the shelf
09

Person baker.person

Someone the posts name, quote or show: the baker, a regular, a winemaker, a speaker. A name or an anonymous role, since when, their reason, the photos that show them, their own words verbatim, and the date they agreed. The file is the consent and holds only what the person agreed to: no name in the file, no name in the post. A page names a person with person:. Withdrawing a person is deleting one file. 

id:     baker
role:   Baker
since:  2019
reason: Quit her agency job and turned the copy shop into a bakery.
consent: 2019-05-04  # no name: she is "the baker" in every post

quote
  What’s gone is gone. Tomorrow there’s more.

05 / How it is used

With me, or without me.

Brand Atelier, with me

Brand Atelier is the implementation I run with clients. I author the files together with the brand, the tool renders posts from them, text generation runs inside the rules, and review, scheduling and publishing follow the brand’s own process. It has just started live testing with a few brands.

The files are the easy part. The work is keeping them true to a brand over the years, extending the format when a brand needs something it cannot say yet, and fitting all of this into how a team decides and approves. That is what I do as designer and consultant, so Brand Atelier is invite only for now.

Write to me

The format, without me

The spec is open and MIT licensed. The parser, the examples and the VS Code extension ship with it. Read the spec, write your own brand, point your own agent at the files. Fork it, build a renderer, use the vocabulary and leave the files.

What I want back is feedback: what you couldn’t say with it, what a parser should have refused, which rule is wrong for your brand. Open an issue, or write.

06 / Try it

Ten minutes with the example brand.

1. Install the VS Code extension

Syntax highlighting, completion, hover docs and validation for all nine file types.

extinstall studiostoll.brand-intent-lang

Or install from the VS Code Marketplace.

2. Run init

Copies the Krume example into your project and installs two skills into your AI agent: /brand-intent to explore, author, generate and validate, and /brief to export a portable markdown brief.

$npx brand-intent init

Works with: Claude Code, Cursor, Gemini CLI, VS Code Copilot, Codex CLI

3. Explore, then write your own

Explore (/brand-intent example). Your agent walks you through the Krume files: why the identity says what it says, how the brand derives from it, what a purpose adds and what it may not.

Generate and validate (/brand-intent daily-bread). Content for a purpose, checked against the voice rules, the pillars, the anti-audiences and the review questions.

Onboard (/brand-intent onboard). A guided interview that drafts your own identity, brand and a first purpose. The result is a first draft to argue with.

Parse. The package exports the TypeScript parser for all nine types, so your own tooling can read the files.
